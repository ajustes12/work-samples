Many home internet connections sit behind a router, which blocks unsolicited connections by default. That does not make a home network invulnerable, but it does mean that a message persuading someone to click or share information is often a more direct route than probing random devices.

## People are part of the picture

Phishing messages try to look routine or urgent so someone will open a link, enter a password, or approve a request. A careful pause can help: check the sender and destination, and use the service's known website or app instead of a link in an unexpected message.

## Keep the basics current

Use unique passwords and MFA where available, install updates for the router and connected devices, and change default router credentials. These steps reduce common risks without requiring special equipment.
